# Case-021: A2G (Alliance Euro-Marocaine de Gériatrie et Gérontologie)

> **Verdict note:** This case carries the impersonation cluster documented in Cases 002, 003, 005, and 006: a real foreign micro-organization with no connection to the advertised role, a wholly generic template description, no corroboration for the role anywhere, and no real company website — the LinkedIn page's website field is a Google Form. Two documented points carry it past Case-010's not-verified grading: the posting was removed from LinkedIn within about a day of going live, matching the rapid-closure record of Cases 002 and 006, and the advertised employer has no website at all rather than a dead one. The association itself is real and is **not** accused of anything: what is graded here is the posting, which the evidence does not connect to the association's actual activity in any way.

## Observation

A LinkedIn posting titled "Junior Cybersecurity Analyst", listed in Ohio, United States as remote and full-time with pay not listed, appeared under the A2G name. On paper the listing fit the investigator's applicant rules: 0–2 years of experience, a bachelor's degree preferred only, Security+ and CySA+ listed as a plus only, and duties that read as genuine SOC and incident-response work. What did not fit was the employer: the named company is a geriatrics and gerontology association, the watch could find no cybersecurity or US operation behind it, and the listed poster was reported at sighting as an Art Director. The job watch logged it UNVERIFIED (LH-10003) and surfaced it; the investigator directed a dig on 2026-11-14.

## Hypothesis

Either a legitimate US hiring employer stood behind the posting under the A2G name, or the posting used a real foreign micro-association's name and page for a role that association has no connection to, following the pattern documented in Cases 002, 003, 005, and 006. Verification checked the posting text, the company page, the named organization in its own country's registry, and the open web for any corroboration of the role.

## Evidence

- **Total industry mismatch.** The named employer is a 2–10 person French geriatrics association — an events and colloquia body for elderly-care medicine between France and Morocco — advertising a US remote cybersecurity analyst role. Nothing in the posting text mentions geriatrics, healthcare, patients, France, Morocco, or events; nothing specific to this employer appears anywhere in it.
- **The organization is real, French, and unrelated to US tech hiring.** The CerfApp registry entry (RNA W751259323) places it at 21 rue de Presbourg, 75116 Paris, created December 2020, in the "Santé et action sociale" category. No hiring, cybersecurity role, or US operation is mentioned there or anywhere else found.
- **No company website exists.** The LinkedIn page's website field is a Google Form, not a domain. No official site for the association was found. The only employee LinkedIn lists for the page is a geriatrician in Montpellier, France — a bureau member of the association — not a recruiter or IT staff.
- **Wholly generic template text.** The full text, recovered from a Trabajo.org mirror ("provided by a partner site"), lists 17 generic duty bullets (SIEM alert monitoring, incident response, vulnerability scans, patch management, firewall/IDS/IPS, phishing training, backups, compliance) and equally generic requirements. The only US-specific line is "must be legally authorized to work in the United States". No pay, no benefits, no city, and no company description are stated.
- **Rapid removal.** The posting was live on 2026-11-13 and already gone from LinkedIn by the 2026-11-14 dig — removed within about a day, matching the rapid-closure pattern of Case-002 (approximately 2 hours) and Case-006 (approximately 7 hours).
- **No corroboration anywhere.** No other posting by this company was found; the mirror was the only copy of the text located. Searches tying the company name to job scams returned nothing — an absence of reports that is not treated as evidence either way; the verdict rests on the mismatch evidence above.
- **No fee or personal-information request in the text.** Nothing in the recovered text asks for money, banking details, or documents — which is why this is graded likely fake rather than a confirmed scam: no harvesting mechanism was observed in operation.
- **Poster identity Not Verified.** The poster was reported at first sighting as an Art Director, but no name was recorded, and the identity could not be corroborated at dig time: public searches found no Art Director linked to this association, and the signed-in LinkedIn checks were unavailable. The poster indicator is therefore recorded as reported-but-unverified and is not load-bearing for the verdict.

## Verification

- Posting review: LinkedIn 4488997606 logged by the job watch 2026-11-13 (LH-10003, UNVERIFIED) — fit on paper, employer legitimacy unverified. At dig time the LinkedIn page returned "This job is no longer available" on repeated checks.
- Full-text recovery: Trabajo.org mirror (us.trabajo.org/job-4112-494bdae2a017925a196d0284dad7061a) — generic template text, no employer-specific content, no pay, no external application site named.
- Company page review: linkedin.com/company/association-a2g — HQ Paris, Events Services, 2–10 employees, Self-Employed, French geriatrics content only, website field is a Google Form, one listed employee (a geriatrician in Montpellier). The page's Jobs subpage sits behind LinkedIn's sign-in wall and could not be checked; nothing in the public page shows any job.
- Registry check: CerfApp French association registry — Alliance Euro-Marocaine de Gériatrie et Gérontologie, RNA W751259323, Paris, health and social-action category, elderly-issues purpose. No hiring or US operation.
- Disambiguation check: A2G International (a2gintl.com, Titusville, Florida) is a different company with the same initials; a search engine's AI overview conflated the two, and that conflation is not evidence about this posting.
- No application attempted; no personal information sent; no party contacted.

## Conclusion

**Likely fake / impersonation — not a confirmed scam.** The posting advertises a US remote cybersecurity role under the name of a real French geriatrics micro-association that has no US presence, no cybersecurity function, no website, and no visible connection to the role, using a wholly generic template, and it disappeared from LinkedIn within about a day. Read against Case-010, which carried the same mismatch cluster and was held at not verified on the investigator's verdict, two documented differences support the stronger grade here: the rapid removal, which matches the closure record of the patterned impersonation cases, and the complete absence of a company website, where the page's website field is a Google Form. What is not established is who created the posting or what applicant information sent through it was used for — and the association itself, a real body doing unrelated work, is not labelled fraudulent. Whether the posting's purpose was information harvesting remains a hypothesis, not a finding.
