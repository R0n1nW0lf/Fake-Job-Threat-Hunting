# Case-008: REVO — Revolutionary Volatility Operations

> **Verdict note:** This case differs from Cases 002, 003, 005, and 006 in one respect: no real business was impersonated, because no business by this name could be found at all. The advertised identity appears fabricated wholesale. The verdict is "likely fake" on the strength of that total absence combined with a generic template posting — recorded as an assessment of the evidence found, not as proof of who created the posting or why.

## Observation

A full-time posting titled "Cyber Security Consultant", listed as Miami, FL and tagged Remote, appeared under the company name "REVO — Revolutionary Volatility Operations". The description is a generic consulting template: vulnerability assessments, security control reviews, threat landscape analysis, incident response support, security reports, and policy and architecture work. Qualifications are equally generic (cybersecurity fundamentals, network security, application security, vulnerability assessment, NIST / ISO 27001 / CIS familiarity); a bachelor's degree or equivalent experience is listed, and certifications such as CISSP, CISM, CEH, and OSCP are named as "a plus" only. No salary is listed. No job poster or hiring team member is named anywhere on the posting. The company name on the posting is plain, non-clickable text. The posting's own company description paragraph describes a "technology-driven organization focused on managing and securing complex, high-volatility digital environments" in generic terms, with no clients, products, address, or any verifiable detail. At the investigation check, approximately one hour after posting, LinkedIn displayed 7 applicants.

## Hypothesis

The posting may belong to a very new or very small genuine business with no public footprint yet, or the advertised employer may be fabricated and the posting created to collect applicant information through Easy Apply. The investigation set out to find any independent trace of the business, without assuming either.

## Evidence

- **No LinkedIn company page exists.** The company name on the posting is not a link, and a LinkedIn Companies search for the name returns no matching page. Every earlier case in this register had at least a company page behind the posting; this one has none.
- **No web presence exists under the name.** An exact search for "Revolutionary Volatility Operations" returned zero results about any such company — every hit was unrelated trading content about market volatility. Follow-up searches combining the name with LinkedIn, Miami, and cybersecurity returned only unrelated companies and job boards.
- **No business registration was located.** A Florida business-registry (Sunbiz) oriented search returned no entity under this name; the only near match, REVOLV, INC., is an unrelated company based in Roanoke, VA.
- **The role is corroborated nowhere.** No mirror of the posting was found on any other job board (including Dice and Built In Miami cybersecurity listings), and there is no company website or careers page where it could be confirmed.
- **No poster is displayed.** The posting names no job poster and shows no "Meet the hiring team" section; applications route only through LinkedIn Easy Apply.
- **The text is a generic template.** Neither the role description nor the company description contains a single verifiable specific: no client, product, office, founding detail, or named person.

## Verification

- Exact-name web search ("Revolutionary Volatility Operations", in quotation marks): zero results about a company.
- Name + LinkedIn / Miami / cybersecurity searches: no company page, profile, or directory entry found.
- Florida Sunbiz-oriented registration search: no entity under this name found.
- Cross-board checks (Dice, Built In, general search): no copy or mirror of the advertised role found anywhere.
- LinkedIn Companies search: no matching company page exists; the name on the posting is non-clickable text.
- One counter-consideration was checked and weighed: a brand-new business can legitimately have a thin footprint. What weighs against that here is the combination — no company page at all, no website, no registration found, no named human, and a mid-senior consulting role described entirely in template language — rather than any single absence on its own.

## Conclusion

**Likely fake — fabricated employer.** Unlike the impersonation cases, there is no underlying real business whose name was borrowed; no evidence that a business by this name exists was found in any source checked, and the posting carries the register's recurring template and zero-corroboration indicators. The evidence supports "the advertised employer appears fabricated and the posting should not receive personal information". Who created the posting, and for what purpose, is Not Verified; information harvesting is a hypothesis, as throughout this project, not an established finding.

## Action Taken

- Skipped. No application and no personal information submitted.
- Recorded as a third technique variant in the correlation analysis: wholesale fabrication of the employer identity, alongside impersonation of third-party companies (Cases 002, 003, 005, 006, with 001 a copying variant) and unverifiable blind staffing posts (Cases 004, 007).
