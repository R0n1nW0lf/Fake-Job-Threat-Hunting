## Observation

A LinkedIn posting titled "Cyber Security Analyst" appeared under the company name Netrolynx AI, advertised as a remote US role with salary undisclosed. It had been posted approximately 3 hours before it was flagged for verification.

## Hypothesis

The posting may be a copy or composite of legitimate employer material, reposted under a different company name to solicit applicants.

## Evidence

- **Header/body mismatch (observed):** the posting header named Netrolynx AI and advertised a remote role, while the body text was a "Senior Cybersecurity Analyst" description naming YAZAKI North America in Canton, Michigan.
- **Genuine employer identified (finding):** deeper verification identified the genuine role as belonging to EaglePicher Technologies, on-site in Joplin, Missouri — not Netrolynx AI and not remote.
- **Employer-site verification (verification):** the genuine role was confirmed against EaglePicher Technologies' own careers (iCIMS) page on 2026-10-02.
- **Contemporaneous record:** the original LinkedIn URL, job ID, YAZAKI header/body mismatch, EaglePicher identification, and Joplin location are recorded in the dated investigation log for 2026-10-02.
- No application was submitted and no personal information was sent.

## Verification

The posting was not accepted at face value because the advertised company and location did not match the body text. The role was cross-checked against the real employer's careers page. That check supported EaglePicher Technologies as the genuine employer and Joplin, Missouri as the genuine work location, while the LinkedIn posting used the Netrolynx AI name and advertised the role as remote.

### Evidence limitation

No screenshot or preserved side-by-side text-comparison file exists for this case. The "Confirmed scam" verdict rests on the contemporaneous dated investigation log, the recovered original LinkedIn URL and job ID, and the employer-site verification performed on 2026-10-02. The full posting text was not preserved, so the precise relationship between the YAZAKI-named body text and the EaglePicher role cannot now be reconstructed from a preserved comparison file.

## Conclusion

**Confirmed scam.** The contemporaneous investigation documented a posting under a false advertised employer identity, with mismatched body material and a genuine role verified at a different employer and location. The posting was blocked before any submission.

## Action Taken

- Blocked before any application or information was submitted.
- Netrolynx AI was recorded as a known scam source and later postings under the same name were skipped or blocked on sight:
  - 2026-10-04: a "Security Engineer" repost (job-watch #616) that copy-pasted Telos company text.
  - 2026-10-09: a "Senior Cyber Security Analyst" posting (LinkedIn job 4478350052; job-watch #1759) whose text was actually a MANTECH role.
  - A "Cybersecurity Engineer" repost (LinkedIn job 4478061572) was also skipped on sight.
  - An additional early "Cloud Security Engineer" listing under the same name (LinkedIn job 4473936778) appeared in early listings.

## Indicators

- Header/body mismatch: advertised company differed from the company named in the description
- Copied body text was senior-level, despite the posting title omitting "Senior"
- Real on-site role advertised as remote
- Company name did not match the employer that owned the genuine role
- Repeated later postings under the same advertised name copied other companies' text
