# Final Investigation Report

**Project:** Fake Job Recruitment Threat Hunting and OSINT Investigation
**Investigator:** Ronald Wolf, SOC analyst candidate
**Investigation period:** September 30 – November 9, 2026
**Report date:** 2026-10-16 (updated 2026-10-17 to add Case-007; updated 2026-10-20 to add Case-008; updated 2026-10-23 to add Case-009; updated 2026-10-23 to add Case-010; updated 2026-10-24 to add Case-011; updated 2026-10-26 to add Cases 012–015; updated 2026-11-03 to add Cases 016–017; updated 2026-11-09 to add Cases 018–019; updated 2026-11-11 to add Case-020)

## Executive Summary

During a cybersecurity job search, twenty suspicious job postings were investigated using a structured OSINT method: observe, hypothesise, gather evidence, verify against independent sources, then conclude and act. One posting was a confirmed scam that copied a real employer's job. Six postings received likely fake verdicts: four built on multiple independent indicators of impersonation (real foreign businesses with no connection to the role, posters publicly affiliated with unrelated employers, dead or absent domains, and — in Cases 002, 003, 005, and 006 — no corroboration for the role found outside LinkedIn), one (Case-008) where the advertised employer appears fabricated wholesale — no LinkedIn company page, website, business registration, or any other trace of the business could be found — and one (Case-019, investigated 2026-11-09) where an aggregator front's description presented a real employer, Fidelity, as the hiring company while no matching role exists on that employer's own careers board, the description text exists nowhere else on the web, and the apply destination was already expired about an hour after posting. Rapid closure is documented in two of the patterned cases (Case-002 in approximately 2 hours, Case-006 within approximately 7 hours). Whether any posting's purpose was information harvesting is a hypothesis, not an established finding. Thirteen postings could not be verified and were skipped without being labelled fraudulent, because the evidence did not support a stronger verdict; the most recent of these include two further employer-board-absence cases and two mislabeled reposts (Cases 012–015, investigated 2026-10-25), the register's first unstable posting (Case-016, investigated 2026-11-02, whose content changed under a single LinkedIn ID during verification), a swapped-template posting from the same aggregator front as Case-015 (Case-017, investigated 2026-11-03), and a posting carried by a product brand page rather than a company (Case-018, investigated 2026-11-05), and a retitled repost of a real employer's role with its pay range and both required qualifications stripped (Case-020, investigated 2026-11-11). No application was submitted to any of the twenty postings, and no personal information was sent.

## Scope and Methodology

Scope: Cybersecurity job postings encountered during the investigator's own job search between September 30 and November 9, 2026, that presented indicators of impersonation, copying, or information harvesting. Eighteen were sighted on LinkedIn; one (Case-011) was sighted as an aggregator card on RemoteRocketship.

Methodology: each case followed the loop Observation → Hypothesis → Evidence → Verification → Conclusion → Action Taken, guided by the principle: don't assume, gather evidence, question it, analyse it, and verify the source before trusting it. Verification used public web search (including exact-phrase searches), DNS and domain resolution checks, business directories, employer websites and careers pages, and LinkedIn company pages and Jobs tabs. No suspicious party was contacted and no test application was submitted.

## Correlation Findings

Across Cases 002, 003, 005, and 006 the same pattern recurs: a real foreign business's name or page, a generic US-remote cybersecurity template, a poster with no affiliation to the named company (or no poster at all), a dead or absent domain, and no corroboration for the role found outside LinkedIn. On seniority the record is precise rather than uniform: Cases 003, 005, and 006 were junior-titled, while Case-002 was open to 0–3 years of experience. On closure: Case-002 closed approximately 2 hours after going live and Case-006 within approximately 7 hours; Case-003 also closed (interval Not Verified) and Case-005's closure status is Not Verified. Sister postings reusing identical templates were documented in Cases 002, 003, and 005. Case-006 additionally showed two company pages displaying the placeholder name "No aplica"; how that display came about is Not Verified (see Unresolved Questions). Case-001 used a different technique, copying a real employer's job text outright, and its copied body text described a senior role.

The evidence supports a shared pattern and playbook. It does not establish that a single actor operates all of these postings, and no attribution is claimed. Full detail is in `analysis/correlation-analysis.md`.

Case-007 (Nexorant LLC, investigated 2026-10-17) does not extend that pattern. It shares the blind-posting indicators — a generic template naming no end employer, and no corroboration for the role found anywhere — but none of the impersonation indicators: the account appears to be the firm's own, no poster mismatch or copied text was found, and no dead domain was involved. Together with Case-004, it forms a second, distinct category in this register: unverifiable blind staffing posts, recorded as not verified rather than fake.

Case-008 (REVO — Revolutionary Volatility Operations, investigated 2026-10-20) is a third variant. It shares the generic-template and zero-corroboration indicators, but no real business was impersonated and no traceable firm posted it: no LinkedIn company page exists for the advertised employer, no website or business registration under the name was located, and an exact-name web search found no trace of any such business. The employer identity appears fabricated wholesale. Who created the posting, and why, is Not Verified.

Case-009 (Wrench Group, investigated 2026-10-23) is a fourth variant, and the register's first stale-requisition case. The employer is real and the posting ran under its own name, but at verification time its own iCIMS applicant tracking system carried no such opening at all — an unfiltered search showed only an unrelated HVAC Maintenance Technician job, and keyword searches and the IT category filter all returned "no jobs found". The listing text matches an approximately 8-month-old mirror under a different title, and one mirror already records the role as no longer listed. The evidence supports "not verified — skip", not "fake": a closed role recirculating through aggregators, or a sync failure, cannot be excluded, and no impersonation indicator was established.

Case-010 (Spiralation, investigated 2026-10-23) carries the impersonation pattern's indicator cluster — a real foreign business unconnected to the role (a Sri Lankan government startup-accelerator program with no US presence), a poster publicly affiliated elsewhere, a listed company website that does not resolve, and no corroboration for the role outside LinkedIn — but it is recorded as not verified, not likely fake, on the investigator's verdict. The evidence establishes a total employer/job mismatch and the absence of any legitimate US hiring employer behind the posting; it does not establish who created the posting or why, and the verdict is not upgraded on the strength of the cluster alone.

Case-011 (DISCO, investigated 2026-10-24) is a second instance of the Case-009 variant, and the register's first sighting from an aggregator rather than LinkedIn. A RemoteRocketship card advertised a "Security Engineer" role under DISCO's name as fully remote with pay undisclosed, the full description behind a sign-up wall. DISCO's own careers board, read in full across every department, carries no "Security Engineer" and no security-titled role of any kind, and its standalone Greenhouse board is deactivated, so the checked embedded board is the only official board where the role could appear. The employer is real and the card ran under its own name; no impersonation indicator was established. Whether the card was stale, mislabeled, or pointed to a posting that never was a live DISCO requisition is Not Verified, and the evidence supports "not verified — skip", not "fake".

Cases 012 and 014 (V2Soft and Blue Mantis, both investigated 2026-10-25) are further instances of the Case-009/011 variant. In both, the advertised role had no presence on the poster's or employer's own careers board at verification time — V2Soft's board listed 150 jobs with no Network Security Engineer, and Blue Mantis's board listed 37 jobs with no Security Analyst L1 — and in both the LinkedIn posting itself had already been removed, so no posting text survives to verify against. Case-012's poster is a staffing firm whose posting named no client employer; Case-014's listing additionally contradicted itself on location. No impersonation indicator was established in either case, and the evidence supports "not verified — skip", not "fake".

Cases 013 and 015 (Torentify / Innodata and TaskVerse / FetchJobs, both investigated 2026-10-25) form a fifth variant, recorded here for the first time: the mislabeled repost. In both, a third-party front republished a real employer's job under its own name and the repost misrepresented the work. Case-013's poster is a small repost portal in Indore, India, with no partnership with the named employer found anywhere; its card advertised a full-time remote cybersecurity role, while the employer's own posting for the matching work describes part-time, project-based AI-training contributor work at $15/hr. Case-015's poster is a storefront for a job-board aggregator; its body paraphrases CrowdStrike's real Systems Engineer II role (R29877) with the experience requirement and pay stripped out, and the duties are production-systems / SRE work, not cybersecurity analysis. In both cases the underlying employer and its real posting are identifiable, no impersonation of the employer's own site was documented, and whether either repost was authorized is Not Verified. The verdict in both is "not verified — mislabeled repost", not "fake".

Case-016 (KGS Technology Group, investigated 2026-11-02) joins the Case-009/011/012/014 employer-board-absence family — KGS's own careers page listed 8 roles with no Cyber Security opening — and adds an indicator new to this register: the posting's content changed materially under a single LinkedIn ID during verification, from a broad multi-level version offering contract-to-hire to a Junior Analyst version aimed at December 2026 graduates. Combined with a self-contradictory location and no salary in either version, the evidence best fits a graduate-pipeline resume pool rather than one current opening. The employer is real, no impersonation indicator was established, and the verdict is "not verified — unstable posting / graduate-pipeline resume pool", not "fake".

Case-017 (TaskVerse, investigated 2026-11-03) is the register's first repeat-actor case: the poster is the same aggregator front as Case-015, carrying a "Cyber Security Engineer" posting whose body is a generic template with its company section swapped — the identical title and template under TaskVerse for Germany describes apetito AG, a family-owned food company founded in 1958. The US posting stated it was not currently accepting applications, TaskVerse's own careers page lists no openings (only a "Send Resume" prompt), and Leidos — the employer the boilerplate is associated with — carries no matching role on its own board. The verdict is "not verified — swapped template / aggregator front", not "fake": the established facts are the template rotation and the absent opening, and authorization is Not Verified.

Case-018 (I'm green bio-based, investigated 2026-11-05) is the register's first brand-page case. The advertised employer is not a company but Braskem's "I'm green" bioplastic brand page, whose attached employees are all in marketing; Braskem's own job board lists five openings, all chemistry and sales, with no security role; the posting accepted no applications; and the poster is publicly a PhD student in coastal ecosystem economics in Ireland with no tie to Braskem, recruiting, or cybersecurity, leaving direct messages to him as the only funnel. The verdict is "not verified — brand page / no employer opening", not "fake", on the same discipline as Case-010: a total employer/job mismatch is established, but who created the posting and why is not.

Case-019 (Superworq, investigated 2026-11-09) is the first aggregator case graded likely fake. The poster is a 2–10 person software shop running a bulk job board (89 unrelated postings all dated the same day), while the description presented the role throughout as a Fidelity hire and never named the poster. Fidelity's own careers board carries no matching role, the description text exists nowhere else on the web and misdescribes the employer it names, the card's title and body disagree about the role, and the external apply destination was already expired about an hour after posting. The verdict is "likely fake — aggregator front / false employer body", not a confirmed scam: who wrote the posting, and what applicant information sent to the poster is used for, is Not Verified.

Case-020 (TaskVerse, investigated 2026-11-11) is TaskVerse's third appearance in this register, after Cases 015 and 017, and a third borrowing technique: the retitle. The card advertised a "Cyber Security Engineer" role for Fortress Information Security, but Fortress's own board carries no such role; the real opening behind the card's duties is Security Risk Assessor, a third-party risk (TPRM) job paying $67,832–$94,212 and listed as Orlando HQ Remote, Florida. Fortress's own listing marks at least 2 years of proven experience and an Associate degree as Required; the TaskVerse card omits both, along with the pay. The verdict is "not verified — retitled real role / requirements and pay stripped", not "fake": the underlying role is real, and what fails is the card's fidelity to it. Whether the repost was authorized is Not Verified.

## Impact

The applicant counts documented in these investigations are the counts LinkedIn displayed: 16 applicants displayed at first sighting in Case-002, 26 applicants displayed in Case-003, 26 applicants displayed in Case-006, 3 applicants displayed in Case-007 (whose posting was still open at the investigation check), and 7 applicants displayed in Case-008 (also still open at its investigation check). The displayed counts for Cases 001, 004, 005, 009, 010, 011, 012, 013, 014, 015, 016, 018, and 020 are Not Verified. Case-017's posting displayed 7 clicks at observation time — clicks are not applications, and that figure is not recorded as an applicant count. Case-019's posting displayed 58 clicks at observation time, recorded the same way. Case-020's posting displayed 23 clicks at dig time, recorded the same way. Case-010's posting displayed "Be among the first 25 applicants" — that is a display prompt, not an applicant count, and it is not recorded as one.

A displayed applicant count does not prove that any applicant's information was received, stored, or misused. It is not known how many complete applications were submitted through any of these postings, what information each applicant sent, or what happened to it afterwards. If a posting was fraudulent, applicants who applied through it could have exposed their resumes and contact details to an unknown party — that exposure is a **potential risk (hypothesis)**, not a measured outcome, and no total of affected people can be supported by the available evidence. The investigator's own exposure was zero, because verification preceded every application decision.

## Unresolved Questions

- Who operates these postings and pages. No evidence gathered supports attribution to any individual or group.
- Whether the real firms' LinkedIn pages were compromised, renamed by an insider, or imitated by lookalike pages. The evidence differs by case. For Case-006, the hypothesis that the genuine page was renamed or hijacked without authorization is **Not Verified**: the confirmed observations are the placeholder display name and its mismatch with the URL slug, and there is no direct evidence of compromise, unauthorized renaming, or of who controls the page. Alternatives such as a platform data error or repurposing of an abandoned page cannot be excluded.
- What applicants who did apply were later contacted for, and whether any information they submitted was received or used for further phishing or fraud. No applicant outcome data is available, so this remains unknown rather than assumed.
- Whether the Case-004 firm is a legitimate business with poor presentation or something else. It remains unverifiable on current evidence.
- Whether the Case-007 posting was a genuine client role posted blind by the staffing firm, or something else. The end employer is unnamed and the role is uncorroborated, so it remains unverifiable on current evidence; the firm's inconsistent HQ claims (Moline, IL vs Chicago, IL) are documented but unexplained.
- Whether any business at all stands behind the Case-008 advertised name. No trace of one was found in any source checked; whether the name was invented for this posting or belongs to an unregistered, unlisted operation cannot be determined from public evidence.
- Whether the Case-009 role was ever a live Wrench Group opening that closed before verification, or a requisition that recirculated without the employer's involvement. The employer's ATS carried no trace of it at verification time, and public evidence cannot distinguish between a filled-and-removed opening, a sync error, and an unauthorised repost.
- Who created the Case-010 posting under the Spiralation name, and whether the real program's name and page were used with or without authorization. Public evidence establishes the employer/job mismatch, the dead listed domain, and the poster's lack of affiliation, but not the mechanism, the actor, or the purpose behind the posting.
- Whether the Case-011 aggregator card was stale, mislabeled, or pointed to a posting that never was a live DISCO requisition. The employer's own careers board carried no trace of the role at verification time, and the card's description could not be read behind the sign-up wall, so public evidence cannot distinguish those possibilities.
- Whether the Case-012 and Case-014 roles were ever live openings that closed before verification, and — for Case-012 — which client employer, if any, stood behind the staffing firm's posting. Both LinkedIn postings had been removed by dig time and neither role appeared on the firm's own board, so public evidence cannot distinguish a filled-and-removed opening from a listing that never had a live requisition behind it.
- Whether the Case-013 and Case-015 reposts were authorized by the employers whose work they republished (Innodata Inc. and CrowdStrike). No partnership or authorization evidence was found for either poster, and what the reposters gain from the republication — traffic, signups, applicant flow — remains a hypothesis, not an established finding.
- Why the Case-016 posting's content changed under its own LinkedIn ID during verification, and whether a live KGS opening ever stood behind either version. The change was observed directly, but who made it, and whether the posting was a standing graduate-pipeline collection point, cannot be determined from public evidence.
- Who operates the Case-017 template rotation under the TaskVerse name, whether TaskVerse/FetchJobs is authorized to carry any employer's postings, and what the front gains from them. TaskVerse now appears in two cases (015 and 017) with two different borrowing techniques; the pattern at the poster level is documented, the operator and purpose are not.
- Who created the Case-018 posting under the "I'm green bio-based" brand page, and whether the poster or anyone else received resumes or contact details through it. Public evidence establishes the brand-page mismatch and the poster's lack of affiliation, but not the mechanism, the actor, or the purpose.
- Who wrote the Case-019 description, whether Fidelity is aware its name is being used on the poster's board, and what applicant information sent through Superworq's other listings is used for. The mismatch, the absent Fidelity opening, and the dead destination are documented; the operator's purpose is not.
- Whether TaskVerse is authorized to repost Fortress Information Security roles, and what applicant information sent through the Case-020 card is used for. The retitle and the stripped pay and requirements are documented; authorization and purpose are not. TaskVerse now appears in three cases (015, 017, and 020); the pattern at the poster level is documented, the operator is not.

## Recommendations

**For job seekers**

- Verify the role on the employer's own website before applying.
- Check the poster's affiliation, the company page's Jobs tab, and the industry and location shown on the page itself.
- Resolve the contact domain; treat a dead domain as a stop sign.
- Phrase-search the description; treat employer-free template text as a red flag.
- Treat postings that close within hours as suspect, and never submit personal information before verification.

**For platforms**

- Detect and review company pages displaying placeholder values, and jobs whose industry, location, and company size contradict the page that carries them.
- When a posting is confirmed fraudulent, notify every applicant who submitted information through it.

**For impersonated businesses**

- Monitor your company page name, Jobs tab, and job-board presence, especially if your business is small and outside the advertised industry. Report impersonation promptly and keep a public note on your own website stating where genuine vacancies are posted.
