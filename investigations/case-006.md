## Observation

A full-time remote posting for a "Junior Cybersecurity Analyst", listed in Pennsylvania, US, appeared under a company whose displayed name was literally "No aplica". The job description was a generic template that named no company, no contact email or phone, no pay, and no website. No job poster was displayed.

## Hypothesis

The posting may be running on a real foreign firm's LinkedIn company page that has been renamed to a placeholder, used as a shell for a US-remote template posting.

## Evidence

- The company page (linkedin.com/company/gestiuno-servicios-legales-s-c/) displays the name "No aplica", while its URL slug names the real firm, Gestiuno Servicios Legales, S.C.
- The page shows: Law Practice industry; headquarters San Pedro Garza Garcia, Nuevo Leon, Mexico; 0–1 employees; no website; legal and real-estate specialties.
- The posting itself contained no company name, contact details, pay, or website anywhere in the text.
- 26 applicants were shown, and the posting closed within approximately 7 hours.

## Verification

- The real Gestiuno Servicios Legales, S.C. exists in Mexican business directories as a small law office, with no cybersecurity offering, no US operations, and no careers page.
- Exact-phrase web searches for two distinctive lines from the description ("motivated and detail-oriented Junior Cybersecurity Analyst" and "develop practical experience while working alongside experienced IT and cybersecurity professionals") returned zero results anywhere. The role is corroborated nowhere outside the LinkedIn posting.
- A second LinkedIn company page (slug "catelca", an IT services firm in Panama) is also displayed as "No aplica", showing that multiple real companies' pages carry the same placeholder renaming.

## Conclusion

**Likely fake / impersonation.** A real Mexican law firm's LinkedIn page, displaying a placeholder name, was used as a shell for a generic US-remote cybersecurity template posting that closed within hours after collecting 26 applicants. This case shows the mechanism more clearly than the earlier ones: the shell is a genuine company's page, renamed.

Note on an open question: the page slug matches the real firm's name, which suggests the genuine page itself may have been renamed or hijacked rather than a lookalike page being created. That is a hypothesis. It is not proven by the available evidence.

## Action Taken

- Not applied. No personal information submitted.
- Any future posting under a "No aplica" company name is flagged as this scam pattern on sight.
