## Observation

A LinkedIn card under Torentify's name advertised a full-time, 100% remote role whose body named a different company — Innodata Inc. — as the employer, with duties described as LLM testing with red- and blue-team work and no required years, certifications, or clearance. The two-name mismatch held it for Ronnie's call instead of a blind application.

## Hypothesis

Either Torentify is an authorized channel for a genuine Innodata cybersecurity role, or it is a repost portal dressing up a different kind of work under a cybersecurity, full-time label. Verification checked Innodata's own posting for the matching role.

## Evidence

- **The poster is a repost portal, not the employer.** Torentify is a 2–10 person job portal based in Indore, India, that reposts other companies' jobs under its own name. No partnership with Innodata shows up anywhere.
- **The real work contradicts the card on three points.** Innodata's own posting for the matching role states, in its own terms, that it is not a traditional full-time role: it is part-time, flexible, project-based contributor work at $15/hr.
- **The duties are AI training, not cybersecurity.** Rating model outputs, labeling, ranking, and rewriting. The red-team wording from the card never appeared in any Innodata listing.
- **Three applicant rules fail at once on the real posting:** gig arrangement (not full-time direct), $15/hr pay, and non-cybersecurity duties.

## Verification

- Poster identity check: Torentify's size, location (Indore, India), and repost-portal model established 2026-10-25; no Innodata partnership found.
- Employer-site check: Innodata's own matching posting read at dig time — part-time/project-based, $15/hr, AI-training duties.
- No application attempted; no personal information sent.

## Conclusion

**Not verified as an authorized posting — mislabeled repost. Not proven to be a data-harvesting scam.** What is established is narrower and sufficient: the card's full-time cybersecurity framing does not survive contact with the employer's own posting. This is the platform-farming pattern — reposted jobs as bait for traffic and signups — documented in Ronnie's 2026-10-25 LinkedIn education post.
