# Case-010: Spiralation

> **Verdict note:** This case carries the indicator cluster behind this register's impersonation verdicts — a real foreign business unconnected to the advertised role, a poster publicly affiliated elsewhere, a listed company website that does not resolve, and a posting that exists only on LinkedIn. It is nevertheless recorded as "not verified", not "likely fake", on the investigator's verdict. The evidence establishes a total mismatch between the advertised employer and the advertised job; it does not establish who created the posting or why, and Spiralation is **not** labelled fraudulent in this investigation.

## Observation

A full-time posting titled "Cybersecurity and Information Assurance Specialist", listed in Michigan, United States with the work arrangement shown as "Remote / Hybrid" and no office or site named, appeared under the Spiralation name with pay not listed. The duties mixed genuine analyst work — monitoring security events, assisting in investigating incidents, and vulnerability management and remediation tracking — with GRC-heavy content (policies, audits, compliance, awareness training). Experience was worded as "0–3 years of experience in cybersecurity, IT risk management, information assurance, or a related area", a degree appeared as Preferred, and certifications (Security+, ISC2 CC, SSCP) appeared under Preferred Qualifications only, with no clearance requirement and a "Must be legally authorized to work in the United States" line. On the listing alone, nothing failed the investigator's applicant-fit rules — the stated years band is met at 0 years, the certifications and degree are preferred only, and undisclosed pay is acceptable — which is why it was sent for employer-legitimacy verification before any application, per the standing verify-first rule.

## Hypothesis

The posting may be a genuine US hiring action that happens to share a name with the Sri Lankan program — or it may use a real foreign program's name and page for a role that program has no connection to, following the pattern documented in Cases 002, 003, 005, and 006. The investigation set out to determine whether any legitimate US hiring employer stood behind the posting, without assuming either explanation.

## Evidence

- **The advertised employer is a real foreign government program unconnected to the role.** ICTA's own page (icta.lk/projects/digital-industry/spiralation) confirms Spiralation is its support initiative for tech start-ups, supporting tech entrepreneurs and early-stage firms in Sri Lanka. No US office, no Michigan footprint, and no security-sector activity was found.
- **The LinkedIn company page contradicts the advertised role.** The page lists industry "Fundraising", Type: Government Agency, Founded 2010, HQ Colombo, Western, Sri Lanka, and lists only 1 employee — fields that describe the Sri Lankan program, not a Michigan cybersecurity employer.
- **The listed company website does not resolve.** spiralation.com, the website listed on the LinkedIn company page, returned a DNS failure at verification time.
- **The poster has no affiliation with the advertised employer.** The listed poster, "Niketan Gobbur", shows a headline of an ETL/Informatica data-engineering developer — not an HR or recruiting account — with no affiliation with Spiralation or Sri Lanka ICT shown.
- **The posting exists only on LinkedIn.** No company careers page or applicant tracking system carries it; the program has no jobs portal, and icta.lk carries no Michigan cybersecurity posting. No corroboration for the role was found outside LinkedIn.
- **The work arrangement cannot be confirmed as remote-US-eligible.** The posting states "Remote / Hybrid" but names no office or site, so whether the role is fully remote within the US could not be confirmed.
- **No applicant-fit rule failed on the listing's own text.** The 0–3 years band is met, certifications (Security+, ISC2 CC, SSCP) and the degree sit under Preferred Qualifications only, no clearance is required, the employment type is stated as Full-Time, and pay is not listed (acceptable under the investigator's rules). The verdict in this case rests on employer legitimacy, not on applicant fit.

## Verification

- ICTA official page review (icta.lk/projects/digital-industry/spiralation) — performed live on 2026-10-23; confirms the program's real purpose and Sri Lankan base.
- LinkedIn company page review — industry, type, founding, headquarters, and employee-count fields reviewed live on 2026-10-23.
- DNS / domain resolution check on spiralation.com — does not resolve (2026-10-23).
- Poster public profile affiliation check — headline and affiliation reviewed live on 2026-10-23; no Spiralation or ICTA affiliation shown.
- Corroboration search — no company careers page, ATS listing, or independent source tying this role to a legitimate US hiring employer was found (2026-10-23).
- The verification stopped before any application form was opened: nothing was uploaded and no personal information was sent.

## Conclusion

**Not verified — skip. Not proven fake.** The indicator cluster matches the impersonation pattern documented in Cases 002, 003, 005, and 006: a real foreign business unconnected to the role, a poster publicly affiliated elsewhere, a dead listed domain, and no corroboration outside LinkedIn. What the evidence does not establish is who created this posting or for what purpose, and on the investigator's verdict the case is recorded at the weaker label. What is established is sufficient for the decision: there is no independent evidence tying this posting to a legitimate US hiring employer, so there was nothing verified to apply to. Whether any posting's purpose was information harvesting remains a hypothesis, not a finding.

## Action Taken

- Skipped. No application and no personal information submitted.
