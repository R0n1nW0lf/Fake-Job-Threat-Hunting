# Case-011: DISCO

> **Verdict note:** This case follows the pattern of Case-009. The advertised employer is real, the aggregator card ran under the employer's own name, and no impersonation, fabricated identity, or blind staffing indicator was established. What could not be verified is the role itself: at verification time the employer's own careers board carried no such opening — indeed, no security-titled role of any kind. The verdict is "not verified", not "fake" — a stale or mislabeled aggregator card is a documented possibility, and DISCO is **not** labelled fraudulent in this investigation.

## Observation

A listing titled "Security Engineer" appeared as a card on the RemoteRocketship aggregator under the DISCO name, claiming fully remote work with pay undisclosed. The card exposed only the title, the company name, the remote claim, and the undisclosed pay: the full description sat behind a sign-up wall, so the duties, the employment type, and the requirements could not be read from the card. DISCO is a real company, and nothing on the card itself could be checked against the investigator's applicant-fit rules, which is why it was sent for employer-site verification before any application, per the standing verify-first rule. This is the first sighting in this register that came from an aggregator rather than from LinkedIn.

## Hypothesis

The card may reflect a genuine DISCO opening that had not yet synced to, or had already been removed from, the employer's own careers board — or it may be a stale or mislabeled aggregator card for a posting that never was a live DISCO requisition. The investigation set out to determine whether a live requisition existed on the employer's own system, without assuming either explanation.

## Evidence

- **The employer's own careers board carries no such opening (controlling check).** DISCO's official careers board (csdisco.com/careers) was read in full on 2026-10-24. Its complete listing across all departments (Customer Experience, Engineering, Finance, Legal, Operations, Product Management & Design, Product Strategy, and Sales) contains no "Security Engineer" — and no title containing "Security" appears anywhere in the listing.
- **A text search of the board found no security role.** Searching the board's full text for "Security" returned zero job hits; the only match was a footer link to the company's trust center.
- **The employer's standalone board is deactivated.** DISCO's standalone Greenhouse board (job-boards.greenhouse.io/csdisco) is no longer active; DISCO serves its jobs only through the Greenhouse board embedded on csdisco.com — the board checked above — so there is no second official board where the role could be listed.
- **The full description was never publicly readable.** The RemoteRocketship card exposed only the title, company name, remote claim, and undisclosed pay; the description sat behind a sign-up dialog, so the role's duties, employment type, and requirements could not be read from the card either.
- **The employer is real and the card used its own name.** Unlike Cases 002, 003, 005, and 006, no third-party business was impersonated, and unlike Case-008, the business behind the advertised name is readily traceable.

## Verification

- Employer-site check (controlling): csdisco.com/careers full listing read live on 2026-10-24; every department reviewed; no "Security Engineer" and no security-titled role present.
- Board text search: "Security" returned zero job hits (footer trust-center link only).
- Standalone ATS board check: job-boards.greenhouse.io/csdisco is deactivated; the embedded board on csdisco.com is the employer's only live job board.
- No account was created on RemoteRocketship to pass the sign-up wall; the gated description was not accessed.
- The verification stopped before any application was attempted: no form was opened, nothing was uploaded, and no personal information was sent.

## Conclusion

**Not verified — skip. Not proven fake.** The advertised role could not be confirmed as a live opening anywhere on the employer's own system, and the strongest available explanations on the documented evidence are that the aggregator card was stale, was mislabeled, or pointed to a posting that never was a live DISCO requisition. Those explanations are recorded as possibilities, not as established findings — a genuine opening that was filled and removed before verification cannot be excluded on public evidence. What is established is narrower and sufficient for the decision: with no live requisition on the employer's own careers board, there was nothing verified to apply to.

## Action Taken

- Skipped. No application and no personal information submitted, and no aggregator account created.
- Recorded in the correlation analysis as a second instance of Case-009's stale / unverifiable-requisition variant, with one extension: the unverifiable listing surfaced on an aggregator card (RemoteRocketship) rather than on LinkedIn.
